# capscope report on BoneSAGEPooled — junior read-through (round 1)

Header facts I anchored on: 153.5k params, base loss 0.1749 on the sweep batches, no header warning of any kind (no "undertrained checkpoint", no "no dataflow edges"). The validation score is 0.977 and plateaued; I have not seen the training loss, which the guide says to check first ("if training loss is low while validation is flat, no amount of width will help").

How I decided to weigh the numbers, based only on what the guide says:
- The guide says the truncation sweep is the only measurement that asks the loss, and to trust it over the activation ranks. So for every module I looked first at (a) used rank from the sweep, (b) marginal pressure (relative loss rise when the weight is cut to used−1 directions), (c) the weight rank cap and upstream carry (whether the number is really about this module or about what feeds it).
- Then I used alpha (whether the layer has trained at all), dead/duplicate units and CKA (whether width is wasted), and Dirichlet energy (whether message passing collapsed the nodes) as vetoes.
- I could not see any chart curve: the dump only contains axis tick labels, so every "read the sweep chart" step in the guide I had to replace with the two numbers (used rank, marginal pressure) the chart is annotated with.

## Decisions

1. **gcn.convs.0.lin_l and gcn.convs.0.lin_r (the two 15 → 128 Linears of the first SAGE layer)**
   (a) Decision: leave their width alone. Do not widen. The thing to change is the input: add per-joint input features (there are only 15).
   (b) Numbers: width/input = 128/15 for both; used rank 15 of 15 (max rank 15) for both; weight rank cap "input-capped (≤15)"; upstream carry 15; marginal pressure +17% (lin_l) and +486% (lin_r); loss at rank 1 = 3.11 (lin_l) and 6.82 (lin_r) vs base 0.1749; alpha 8.78 (lin_l) and 6.46 (lin_r), both flagged "undertrained" in the panel badge; lin_l has 30 duplicate units (23%), lin_r 8 (6%); CKA between the two is 0.952; activation rank@99% energy is 9 for both, effective rank 8.2 / 9.3.
   (c) How the tool says they are produced: used rank = smallest k such that replacing the weight by its rank-k SVD approximation keeps the loss within +1% of base (grid then bisection). The rank cap comes from the weight shape [128 × 15]: rank ≤ min(in, out) = 15. Carry = narrowest width along the feeding paths; a source module carries its own input width, here 15. Marginal pressure = relative loss increase at rank used−1. Alpha = Hill fit on the top tenth of eigenvalues of WᵀW. Duplicates = output columns with |corr| > 0.95 to an earlier column. CKA = linear CKA between the two modules' sampled activations.
   (d) Reasoning: the loss needs every one of the 15 directions the weight can have (used = max = 15) and, for lin_r, losing the last one multiplies the loss by ~6. That says the raw 15-dimensional input is fully used, not that 128 outputs are too few: the weight can never have more than 15 directions however wide the output is, so widening here cannot add anything. The activation ranks (99% of variance in 9 directions out of 128) agree that the outputs live in a tiny subspace. The 23% duplicated units and CKA 0.95 between lin_l and lin_r say the 128 output units mostly carry copies of the same 15 things; if anything these layers could be narrower, not wider. Alpha > 6 would normally mean "not trained yet", but the guide says alpha from matrices with fewer than ~50 eigenvalues (these have 15) is "indicative only", the verdict engine did not fire undertrained (upstream comes first in the order), the header did not warn, and loss at rank 1 is 18–40× the base loss, which is the opposite of the guide's undertrained signature ("loss at rank 1 near the base loss"). So I discount the alpha reading. The difference between lin_l (+17%) and lin_r (+486%) I cannot fully explain (see unclear #7): if lin_r is the self-feature path, it means the joint's own raw features are far more load-bearing than the neighbour-mean features.
   (e) Confidence: high that widening is pointless; medium that "add input features" is the correct fix (the tool describes the current model, it does not predict that extra features will move the plateau).

   (b) Numbers: used rank 15 of 15, input-capped ≤15, carry 15, marginal pressure +647%, loss at rank 1 = 15.1; activation effective rank 16.6, rank@99% = 17, numerical rank 30; 2% duplicates; Dirichlet 0.374 (309% of lin_l's 0.121); alpha 7.62 (median of two 15-eigenvalue matrices).

   (d) Reasoning: same as item 1. The output of the sum carries about 17 directions (99% energy) out of 128, consistent with two rank-15 maps of related things being added. Nothing is oversmoothed (energy 0.374 is far above the 0.05 rule and it is rising, not decaying, relative to lin_l). The layer is doing its job on a 15-dim input; the input is the limit.
   (e) Confidence: high (leave alone).

## Overall recommendation

I would not add width anywhere: every full-width weight in the model needs at most ~two-thirds of its directions, and in every case the loss rises only 1–2% when the last needed direction is removed, which the guide says is the signature of a module the loss is not pressing on. The only place the loss is genuinely pressed is the first SAGE layer, and there the constraint is the 15-dimensional raw input (used 15 of a maximum 15, +486%/+647% for losing one direction, carry 15), which no width can fix. So the first change I would make is to the input features rather than the architecture: add more per-joint features (and, if the colleague has them, features that describe the joint's place in the rig), then re-run capscope to see whether layer 0's used rank rises with the input width and whether pressure appears further down. In parallel I would check the training loss (if it is also plateaued at ~0.17 this is capacity/optimisation, if it is much lower the plateau is generalisation) and measure the head-gradient cosine on the trunk that the guide suggests, since a multi-head plateau with no capacity signal is consistent with head conflict. If parameter count matters, convs.3 (34–50 used of 128) and possibly trunk.2 are where I would try narrowing, expecting no loss change.

## Unclear parts of the GUI

1. **Truncation sweep chart (every module panel, "Loss under SVD truncation").** The dump contains only axis ticks and the annotation line; the curve itself is not in the text. The guide's step "confirm each with its sweep chart: the used-rank marker should sit near the right with the loss climbing steeply to its left" therefore could not be done. Question: is there a way to get the sweep's (k, loss) table as numbers, so the curve can be read without the chart?
2. **Capacity verdict inputs, "marginal pressure" vs the +1% tolerance.** Used rank is defined as the smallest k within +1% of base, so the loss at used−1 must be at least ~1% above base by construction. Many modules show exactly "+1%". Question: is +1% literally "just outside the band" (i.e. no information beyond the definition), and how far above 1% does pressure need to be before the tool considers it meaningful? The guide contrasts 30% and 1% but gives no threshold and the priority formula only says "up to 0.10 for pressure".
3. **gcn.convs.1: container used rank (61) is lower than its child lin_r's used rank (82).** The container sweep truncates lin_l and lin_r jointly to the same k. I do not understand how truncating both to 61 stays within tolerance when truncating lin_r alone needs 82. Question: does the container sweep use a different grid/bisection endpoint, or can joint truncation compensate in a way single truncation cannot? (The same pattern does not appear elsewhere: convs.2 container 80 ≥ children 70/75; convs.3 container 56 ≥ 34/50.)
4. **Weight spectrum (alpha) badge on gcn.convs.0.lin_l/lin_r/container: "undertrained".** The panel prints the word "undertrained" as a badge, while the guide says alpha from matrices with fewer than ~50 eigenvalues is indicative only, the verdict did not fire undertrained, and the header did not warn. Question: should the badge be suppressed or marked "indicative" for small matrices? As presented, a reader has to know the caveat to discount it. Also, for a [128 × 15] weight, "top tenth" is k = 1 — is a Hill fit through one ratio meaningful at all?
5. **Upstream carry for layers after the first.** convs.1.lin_l has carry 15 but used rank 43 and rank@99% 27, i.e. it clearly holds more than 15 dimensions of information. The guide explains that aggregation "brings in other nodes' features, which carry does not count", so carry is "a floor". Question: after layer 0 in a message-passing model, is carry ever informative, or should I ignore it entirely for GNN layers? Relatedly, the gcn container shows carry 15 while its own last child convs.3 shows 120 — the container line is the source's input width and the child's is the propagated value, but the panel does not say which one is meant.
6. **"used rank" on passthrough modules (trunk.1, trunk.3).** The panel prints "used rank 91 of 128 (71%)" with the generic sweep description, but there is no weight to sweep; the guide says the fallback to rank@99% energy is used "and the panel says so". I could not find where the panel says so. Question: is this number the 99%-energy rank, and if so why is it labelled with the sweep description?
7. **lin_l vs lin_r semantics.** Only the dataflow guide paragraph mentions "a SAGEConv returning lin_l(agg) + lin_r(x)". The panels never say which linear is the neighbour-aggregate path and which is the self path, yet the two show very different pressures in layer 0 (+17% vs +486%). Question: is lin_l = aggregated neighbours, lin_r = the node's own features, and if so is the tool's reading that the joint's own raw features carry most of the loss-relevant information while the neighbour mean carries little?
8. **Dirichlet energy, "GRAPH: OVERSMOOTHING".** gcn.convs.0.lin_l reads 0.121 with no upstream comparison ("–"). The guide says to read the decay, never the level, and the rule needs energy < 0.05 anyway, so nothing fires. But 0.121 on the very first reading looks low against "random ≈ 2", and if lin_l is the neighbour-mean path it is smooth by construction. Question: is a low first reading something to worry about, or is any reading that has no upstream to compare with simply uninformative?
9. **Rank and saturation, "numerical rank vs 90%-energy gap".** The guide says a large gap "is what a module looks like just before it runs out of room". trunk.1 has numerical rank 128 and 90% @ 25; convs.1.lin_r 125 vs 9. By that sentence most of the model looks "about to run out of room", while the sweep says none of it is. The guide does say to trust the sweep, but the two statements sit in adjacent sections without saying which wins in this exact case. Question: is the gap heuristic only meant for use without a sweep?
10. **Module list, "priority".** trunk 0.44, gcn 0.42, convs.1.lin_r 0.40, convs.2 0.39 are all "tight" with +1–2% pressure. The guide says to read the ordering, not the number, but then "take the top three or four by priority" — when the top four are all tight-with-no-pressure, the ordering itself tells me nothing about what to do. Question: is there an intended reading of a list whose top is all low-pressure "tight"? (I concluded "nothing to widen", but I am not sure that is how the author intends the list to be used.)
11. **Loss decomposition per head.** The sweep uses one scalar loss. side_head at rank 1 costs 0.65, binary_head 2.75, region_head 1.51. The panel never says whether the loss is a sum over the three heads or something else, so I cannot tell how much of the 0.1749 base comes from which head. Question: can the sweep report per-head loss?
12. **Activation histogram / activation range for trunk.0 and trunk.2** (std 2.8–3.7, ranges to ±66). The guide only says what a zero spike or bounded-activation pinning means. Question: are these magnitudes (going into a GELU) something the tool considers, or is scale irrelevant to every metric here?
13. **CKA "heatmap".** The guide refers to a heatmap with "dark square blocks along the diagonal", but the dump only has the nearest-neighbour lists. I could not do that check.
14. **Rank cap wording for containers.** gcn shows "width (D) / input dim 128 / 15" but "weight rank cap square (≤128)". The guide explains the container rule (cap comes from the leaf producing the output), but the panel line "input dim 15" next to "square" reads as contradictory until you find that paragraph.

## Things I had to guess

- That lin_l is the neighbour-aggregate path and lin_r is the self path in each SAGEConv (from one guide sentence, not from the panels).
- That "+1%" marginal pressure corresponds to a flat sweep curve to the left of the marker; I could not see the curves.
- That the sweep's loss is the summed three-head training loss, so "loss at rank 1" on a head reflects that head's share.
- That the 15-wide input is the raw per-joint feature vector, and that it is something the colleague can extend (the GUI only says "add input features" / "richer inputs").
- That the 23% duplicate units in convs.0.lin_l are a consequence of 128 outputs being linear functions of a 15-dimensional input rather than a training pathology (my own reasoning, not stated by the tool).
- That narrowing convs.3 or removing trunk.2 would be loss-neutral because their used ranks are low and pressure is +1%; the guide explicitly says no metric predicts what a differently sized model does, so this is an experiment, not a conclusion.
- That the small-matrix caveat on alpha is enough to discount the "undertrained" badges on layer 0, given no header warning and large loss-at-rank-1 values.
- That the three heads' widths (4, 3, 4) match the label spaces (the task description said "binary labels" but the head has 4 outputs; I assumed several binary labels).
