# capscope report on BoneSAGEPooled — what I would change (junior reading, round 2)

Context I am holding fixed: 4 SAGE layers × 128, 2-layer MLP trunk × 128 on concat(joint embedding, rig mean), three heads (4 / 3 / 4 logits), ~153.5k params, base loss 0.1749 on the sweep batches, val 0.977 and plateaued. Header shows no "undertrained" and no "no dataflow edges" warning.

How I read the numbers (so I do not have to repeat it 15 times):
- "used rank k / max" = the tool overwrote the module's weight(s) with their rank-k SVD approximation, re-ran the frozen model on a few batches, and found the smallest k whose loss is within +1% of 0.1749. "marginal pressure" = loss rise at k−1. The (k, loss) table is every point it evaluated.
- "weight rank cap" = min(in, out) of the weight that produces the module's output; a weight cannot pass more directions than that, whatever the output width.
- "upstream carry" = narrowest width on the paths feeding the module, computed as min(in_dim, sum over producers of min(width, carry)). The guide says it is a floor past the first graph layer because message passing brings in other nodes' features that it does not count.
- "rank @ 99% energy" = smallest number of leading singular directions of the mean-centred [4096 × D] activation sample holding 99% of the variance. Activation-only; the guide says trust the sweep over it.
- alpha = Hill power-law fit on the top tenth of the eigenvalues of WᵀW; > 6 means "still looks like random init", 2–6 fine.
- Dirichlet = mean squared difference between neighbouring nodes' (centred) outputs over the mean squared distance to the mean node; the verdict only uses the ratio to the nearest upstream reading, and only fires below 0.05 absolute and 30% of upstream.
- dead/dup = unit columns with ~zero variance / |corr| > 0.95 with an earlier unit. CKA = linear similarity of two modules' activation matrices on the same rows, 1 = same representation up to a linear map.

## Decisions

1. **gcn.convs.0.lin_l, gcn.convs.0.lin_r, gcn.convs.0 (first SAGE layer, 15 → 128)** — (a) Do not widen, do not narrow the weights; the change belongs to the *input features*. (b) used rank 15/15 on all three; the weight is [128 × 15] so its rank is capped at 15 by the 15-dim input ("input-capped (≤15)"); carry = 15 (the raw input width). Marginal pressure: lin_l +17% (k=14 → 0.204), lin_r +486% (k=14 → 1.025), joint container +647% (k=14 → 1.306). loss at rank 1: 3.1 / 6.8 / 15.1 vs base 0.175. Activation rank @ 99% energy is only 9 (leaves) / 17 (container); container numerical rank 30. (c) The sweep replaced each 128×15 weight by its rank-k approximation and re-ran the model; k=15 is the full rank of that weight, so "used 15/15" means every direction the 15 input features supply is needed, and dropping just one blows the loss up by hundreds of percent. (d) The weight cannot have rank above 15 no matter how many output units it has, so adding output width cannot add information; the model is already squeezing all 15 raw input directions, and a couple of them are so important that losing them multiplies the loss by 6–15. To me that says the raw per-joint feature vector is the narrowest point of the whole network and the model is straining against it. So: engineer more / richer per-joint input features (whatever the rig gives: more geometric quantities, relative offsets, etc.), not more width. The lin_l vs lin_r asymmetry (+17% vs +486%) says one of the two input paths (own features vs neighbour mean) carries almost all the loss-relevant signal; the guide says which is which is in the code, not in the report, so I would look at the forward pass before drawing a conclusion from that. I would *not* narrow the 128 outputs even though only ~17 activation directions are live, because the layer is 4k params and the guide says these metrics do not predict what a different model does. (e) Confidence: **high** on "widening here cannot help" (it is a rank-cap fact); **medium** on "richer input features will move the plateau" (the tool cannot promise that; it only says the current inputs are fully used).

2. **gcn.convs.1 (lin_l used 43/128, lin_r 82/128, container 61/128)** — (a) Leave alone (no widening; do not narrow yet). (b) Marginal pressures +2% / +1% / +2%. lin_r's sweep table: k=75 → +1.6%, k=44 → +11.9%, k=25 → +35%; lin_l: k=25 → +7.4%, k=15 → +20%; container: k=44 → +10.5%, k=25 → +54%. alpha 2.65 / 2.82 / 2.74. dead+dup 0–1%. Best unrelated CKA 0.84–0.85. Dirichlet 0.289 for the container (77% of convs.0's 0.374), nowhere near 0.05. (c) Pressures are the loss rise at used−1; the tables are the loss at each truncation rank; alpha from the weight spectrum; CKA on activation rows. (d) lin_r is the single leaf the module list puts third ("tight", 64%), but losing its last used direction costs only +1%, and it can be cut to 75 directions for +1.6% and to 44 for +12%. The guide says a long flat stretch left of the marker means the module could lose many directions — this curve is flat from 75 to 128. The layer is trained (alpha in the 2–6 band), no wasted units, not collapsed onto its neighbours, not oversmoothed. Nothing here is pressing on the width. Because lin_l and lin_r are summed into the same 128-wide output I cannot widen or narrow one without the other, and lin_r needs 82 with everything else intact, so I would not narrow the layer either. (e) Confidence: **high** for "do not widen"; **medium** for "do not narrow" (the joint number 61 hints a narrower layer might do, but the guide says a container's number and a leaf's are different experiments and I do not know which one to trust for a narrowing decision).

3. **gcn.convs.2 (lin_l 70/128, lin_r 75/128, container 80/128 — the "tightest" conv)** — (a) Leave alone; if I were forced to widen one conv layer this is the one, but I would not. (b) Pressures +1% / +1% / +2%. Container sweep: k=75 → +1.5%, k=44 → +10.8%, k=25 → +25%, k=1 → 7.24. alpha 2.51 / 2.66 / 2.58. dead+dup ≤ 3%. Best unrelated CKA 0.75–0.79. Dirichlet 0.431 (container), 0.901 for lin_r, no decay toward 0. (c) Same computations as above. (d) 63% used is the highest in the GCN, but the loss is indifferent to the last several directions (+1.5% at 75 vs +0.8% at 80, and the bisection wobbles: k=78 → 1.4%, k=79 → 1.5%, k=80 → 0.8%). The guide explicitly says: a top group that is all "tight" with +1–2% pressure is an ordering among modules the loss does not care about. I agree with that reading from the tables alone: the curve is flat until ~44 directions, then climbs. A layer under real width pressure would show a steep climb right at the marker. (e) Confidence: **high** for "do not widen".

4. **gcn.convs.3 (lin_l 34/128, lin_r 50/128, container 56/128) — the most under-used layer, candidate to narrow** — (a) Try narrowing this layer (e.g. 128 → 64) or, as a separate experiment, dropping it (3 hops instead of 4). I would not widen it. (b) lin_l sweep: k=15 → +4.7%, k=25 → +3.3%; lin_r: k=25 → +6.2%, k=44 → +2.3%; container: k=44 → +2.3%, k=25 → +11%; loss at rank 1 is still 1.4 / 2.7 / 2.8, so destroying the weights does hurt. Pressure +1% everywhere. alpha 2.6–2.8. 0% dead/dup. CKA: convs.3 ~ trunk.0 0.907, ~ trunk.2 0.900, ~ trunk.1 0.896, ~ trunk 0.812; convs.3.lin_l ~ trunk.0 0.849. Dirichlet 0.537 (125% of convs.2's 0.431) — no smoothing. (c) lin_l "k=15 → +4.7%" means: replace only lin_l's 128×128 weight by a rank-15 matrix, leave everything else intact, and the loss rises less than 5%. CKA numbers are activation similarity to later modules (relatives and downstream excluded from the "best unrelated" field but still shown in the neighbour list). (d) This layer's weights are doing low-rank work: 15–25 directions get within ~5% of the base loss, and jointly 44 directions get within 2.3%. Its output is also linearly very similar (0.90) to the trunk's activations, i.e. the trunk barely reshuffles what convs.3 produces (see item 6). So the fourth hop is the cheapest place to take parameters out. But the guide is explicit that the metrics say nothing about depth vs width or what a smaller model would do, and "loss at rank 1 = 2.77" means the layer is not a no-op, so removing it is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion the report supports. Narrowing to ~64 is closer to what the sweep actually measured (rank-56 joint truncation was within 1%), but even that is a different model once the nonlinearity acts in 64 dims. (e) Confidence: **medium** for "narrow to ~64 will not hurt", **low** for "remove the layer".

5. **gcn as a whole (used 84/128, "tight", 66%)** — (a) Keep width 128; do not add a fifth layer for capacity reasons. (b) Pressure +2%. Joint sweep of all 8 conv weights: k=75 → +2.1%, k=44 → +24%, k=25 → +90%, k=15 → +212%, k=1 → 6.24. Non-monotone: k=9 (4.81) worse than k=5 (4.44) and k=3 (4.32). Median alpha 2.72. Carry into the stack 15; input dim 15; binding weight square 128 (from convs.3). Dirichlet across containers 0.374 → 0.289 → 0.431 → 0.537 (rising, not decaying). (c) The container sweep truncates every conv weight to the same k at once. (d) Squeezing the whole GCN to 75 directions costs 2%; the loss only starts to bite below ~44. Nothing in the stack is width-limited; message passing has not collapsed the nodes together (Dirichlet does not fall toward 0.05 — it goes up). The whole stack is downstream of a 15-wide input, which brings me back to item 1. The stack's used rank (84) being far above the carry (15) matches the guide's note that nonlinearities plus neighbour aggregation let later layers exceed the input width, so I am not reading it as a contradiction. (e) Confidence: **high** for "no widening"; I cannot say anything about depth from this report (the guide says so too).

6. **trunk (trunk.0 Linear 256→128 used 65/128; trunk.1 GELU; trunk.2 Linear 128→128 used 67/128; trunk.3 GELU; container 88/128 "tight", top of the list)** — (a) Do not widen. Consider a leaner trunk (one Linear+GELU instead of two, or 128 → 96) as an experiment. (b) Pressures +1% / +1% / +1%. Container sweep: k=75 → +1.5%, k=44 → +8.2%, k=25 → +37.5%; trunk.0: k=44 → +5.5%, k=25 → +25%; trunk.2: k=44 → +3.0%, k=25 → +14%. alpha 2.99 / 2.39 / 2.69. 0% dead/dup; GELU zero fractions 3% / 8%. trunk.0 is output-capped (weight [128×256], rank ≤128), carry 120. CKA chain: gcn.convs.3 ~ trunk.0 0.907, trunk.0 ~ trunk.1 0.938, trunk.1 ~ trunk.2 0.946, trunk.2 ~ trunk.3 0.928, trunk.0 ~ trunk.2 0.921, gcn ~ trunk 0.812. The passthrough GELUs show "used rank" 91 / 76 but their field says explicitly this is NOT from a sweep, just the 99%-energy activation rank. (c) Sweep and CKA as above; the "tight" label comes from 88/128 = 69% > 60%. (d) The trunk sits at the top of the list only because it has the highest used%, but losing its last used direction costs +1%, cutting it to 75 directions costs +1.5%, and the loss does not climb steeply until below ~44. So there is no width pressure. Separately, the CKA neighbour lists show a block: gcn.convs.3 and all four trunk modules are pairwise 0.81–0.95 similar. The guide says to look for exactly that pattern ("a run of consecutive modules collapsed onto one stage of computation") even though none of the pairs crosses its 0.98 redundant threshold. My reading: the trunk mostly re-expresses the GCN output rather than building new features, so a second trunk layer is probably not buying much — but the sweep says trunk.2's weights matter (rank-1 loss 3.46, rank-25 +14%), and I do not know how to reconcile "representation barely changes" with "weights matter"; see Unclear #6. (e) Confidence: **high** for "do not widen"; **low** for "shrink to one layer".

7. **binary_head (4 logits, used 4/4, pressure +28%), side_head (3 logits, used 2/3, pressure +272%), region_head (4 logits, used 3/4, pressure +534%)** — (a) Leave the heads alone architecturally. (b) Each head is a Linear(128 → 3 or 4), so its weight rank is capped at 3 or 4 by the output ("output-capped"). Sweep tables: binary k=3 → +28.5%, k=1 → 2.75; side k=2 → −0.0% (identical to base), k=1 → 0.65; region k=3 → −1.9% (*lower* than base), k=2 → +534%, k=1 → 1.51. CKA binary_head ~ region_head 0.882; side_head's best CKA is only 0.60 (to trunk.3). alpha 4.0 / 2.0 / 5.05 with "indicative only (3–4 eigenvalues, k=1)". (c) Same truncation sweep, but on a tiny matrix; the "narrow" verdict says width < 8 so the width is fixed by the task and the capacity rules do not apply. (d) These widths are set by the label spaces, so there is nothing to widen. Two observations I can make without the capacity rules: (i) side_head needs only 2 of its 3 directions with zero loss change — plausible for a 3-way head where one direction is a common shift, which I take as fine, not as a defect (guess). (ii) region_head's loss on the sweep batches *improves* when its 4th direction is removed (0.1716 vs 0.1749). That is small (−1.9%) and could be noise, but it is the only place in the report where damaging a weight helps, which hints this head has a slightly harmful direction (overfitting to something?). (iii) binary_head and region_head outputs are 0.88 CKA-similar, i.e. their logits are close to linear functions of each other — the two label sets may be strongly correlated, or the shared trunk feature is dominated by one of them. The guide says the tool cannot see head conflict at all and suggests measuring pairwise cosine similarity of each head's gradient w.r.t. the shared trunk; since the loss_fn is a sum over heads and not decomposed, I cannot tell from here which head is responsible for the plateau. (e) Confidence: **high** for "leave the head widths"; **low** for anything about head conflict (the report has no measurement of it).

8. **Redundant / undertrained modules** — (a) None that the report's own rules support. (b) No module has dead+dup > 35% (max is convs.0.lin_l with 23% duplicate units, 30 of 128 units correlated > 0.95 with an earlier unit); no unrelated CKA ≥ 0.98 (max 0.958 is between the two convs.0 children, which are siblings, and 0.952 lin_l ~ lin_r); every alpha on the 128×128 and 128×256 weights is 2.4–3.0; loss at rank 1 is 1.2–15 on every swept module, versus base 0.175, so no weight is "destroyable for free" (the guide's undertrained signature). The three convs.0 panels do say "α 8.78 / 6.46 / 7.62 · undertrained", but each is tagged "indicative only (15 eigenvalues, k=1)" and the header gives no undertrained warning. (c) Rules as in the guide's verdict table. (d) The convs.0 23% duplicate units are the only sign of waste, and they are explained by the layer having only 15 input directions to spread over 128 outputs — duplicates are unavoidable there, and it is 2k params. I do not read the convs.0 alphas as a real undertraining signal because the guide says values from matrices with < 50 eigenvalues are indicative only, and with k=1 the Hill formula reduces to one ratio of the top two eigenvalues (see Unclear #1). (e) Confidence: **medium**.

## Overall recommendation

I would not touch any width. Every module that the list ranks as a candidate loses at most 2% loss when its last used direction is removed and shows a long flat stretch in its truncation table, and no module is undertrained, dead, duplicated or oversmoothed. The one place the loss is genuinely straining is the 15-dimensional raw input: the first layer needs all 15 input directions and losing one multiplies the loss by up to ~7×. So the first thing I would change is the per-joint input features (add more, richer ones), then retrain at the same architecture; I expect that to move the plateau more than any width change, though the tool cannot promise it. In parallel I would check training loss (if it is also ~0.17-ish flat, this is not a capacity problem) and measure head-gradient cosine similarity on the trunk, since the report cannot see head conflict. Only as a cost-saving experiment I would try convs.3 at 64 (or 3 hops) and a single-layer trunk, expecting roughly unchanged accuracy.

## Unclear parts of the GUI

1. **Module panel gcn.convs.0.* → WEIGHT SPECTRUM (ALPHA), "α 8.78 · undertrained · indicative only (15 eigenvalues, k=1)".** The section headline paints the layer "undertrained" while the verdict is "upstream" and the header has no warning. With k=1 the formula alpha = 1 + k / Σ log(λᵢ/λₖ₊₁) is just 1 + 1/log(λ₁/λ₂) — one ratio of the top two eigenvalues, not a fit to a tail. Question: should I ignore the "undertrained" label entirely for matrices this small, and if so why does the headline still print it in the same style as a real verdict?

2. **gcn.convs.0.lin_l, RANK AND SATURATION vs TRUNCATION SWEEP.** Activation rank @ 99% energy is 9 but the sweep says all 15 weight directions are needed, with the 15th costing +17% (and +486% for lin_r). The guide says "trust the sweep", but does not explain how the loss can depend so strongly on directions that carry < 1% of the output variance. Question: is that expected (small-variance directions can still be decisive for the loss), and is there any way in the panel to see which input feature those directions correspond to?

3. **GRAPH: OVERSMOOTHING, "vs nearest upstream graph layer".** For a SAGEConv container the comparison is to its own child lin_r ("56% of gcn.convs.1.lin_r"), and for the children the comparison is to the previous container. So the ratio alternates between "pre-aggregation vs post-aggregation of the same layer" and "this layer's Linear vs the previous layer's output", which are different questions. Question: is "nearest upstream" meant to follow execution order, and should I only compare container to container (0.374 → 0.289 → 0.431 → 0.537) when judging decay hop by hop?

4. **Module list and priority: leaves and containers mixed.** gcn.convs.1.lin_r is #3 with priority 0.40 ("tight") while its parent gcn.convs.1 is "spare" 0.07 with used 61 < 82. The guide explains that container and leaf sweeps are separate experiments, but not which number I should act on for a width decision when the two disagree — I cannot widen or narrow lin_r without changing the whole layer. Question: for choosing a layer width, is the container number or the max of the leaf numbers the right one?

5. **TRUNCATION SWEEP tables, non-monotone and noisy near the marker.** gcn.convs.1.lin_l: k=39 → +1.4%, 41 → +1.9%, 42 → +2.2%, 43 → +0.8%; gcn container: k=9 (4.81) worse than k=3 (4.32); convs.0 container: k=14 (1.31) worse than k=13 (0.67); region_head rank 3 is *better* than full rank. The guide mentions non-monotonicity but the tolerance band is 1% while the wobble is ±1–2%. Question: what is the batch-to-batch noise of the loss on the sweep batches, and could "used rank" easily be off by 5–10 directions because the bisection latched onto a wiggle? A noise estimate (e.g. loss spread across the sweep batches) would help.

6. **CKA neighbour lists vs the sweep for gcn.convs.3 → trunk.**  Consecutive modules are 0.90–0.95 CKA-similar, and the guide says to look for such blocks, but the same modules have rank-1 losses of 2.8–4.9 and rank-25 costs of 14–37%, i.e. their weights clearly matter. Question: how should I weigh "representation barely changes (CKA)" against "weights matter (sweep)"? Does high CKA between consecutive Linear layers just mean the dominant directions are preserved while the loss lives in small ones? The guide gives the 0.98 threshold for "redundant" but no guidance for a 0.9 block.

7. **DATAFLOW / upstream carry for trunk.0: "fed by gcn.convs.3", input dim 256, carry 120.** The rig-wide mean concatenation is invisible: only one producer is listed, and carry = min(256, 120). I understand the mean is a functional op on the same tensor, but then the "producers are summed" rule never sees the pooled path. Question: is carry = 120 (rather than 240) intended here, and is 120 itself just 2× convs.2's 60 from the "sum of lin_l and lin_r" over-estimate? The carry doubling every layer (15 → 30 → 60 → 120) looks like an artefact of summing two producers that read the same tensor.

8. **Heads: "narrow — capacity metrics do not apply" but the panel still prints "marginal pressure +534% loss · pressed".** Question: is the pressure number on a head meaningful for anything (e.g. as evidence that the head's directions are all load-bearing), or should the field be greyed out like it is for passthrough modules?

9. **Header "base loss 0.1749" and sweep "loss_fn is a sum over heads, not decomposed".** I cannot tell which head contributes what to the base loss or which head is on the plateau. Question: could the panel show the per-head loss at base and at used−1, since it already runs the forward pass?

10. **Module list "used" column, e.g. "15/128 +486%" for gcn.convs.0.lin_r.** The list shows a 486% "pressure" next to 12% "used", which looks alarming until you open the panel and see the rank cap. The Reading line does explain it, but the column itself does not say "input-capped". Minor.

11. **gcn.convs.0 headline sentence: "add capacity where that information is created (earlier layers, more hops or richer inputs)".** There are no earlier layers; the leaf sentences say "add input features instead", which is clearer. Minor boilerplate confusion.

12. **Activation range on heads: binary_head −100.7 … 49.8, std 10.9.** The guide says scale enters no metric and the histogram is a sanity check. Question: is a logit range of −100 something the tool considers worth flagging (as a sign of over-confident outputs), or should I ignore it here?

13. **"exec #" and "container that computes its own output" vs "container".** gcn is a plain "container" and gcn.convs.3 ~ gcn CKA = 1.000 because gcn returns convs.3's output; trunk likewise returns trunk.3. Not unclear once read, but the CKA heatmap top pairs start with two 1.000 entries that are trivial, which is distracting.

## Things I had to guess

- That "widen the input features" is the actionable consequence of "used 15/15, input-capped, +486%/+647% pressure". The tool says the input is the limit; it does not say new features would be used or would move the plateau.
- That the lin_l / lin_r asymmetry (+17% vs +486%) means one of the self / neighbour-mean paths carries most of the loss-relevant signal. The guide says the tool cannot tell which is which; I would need the forward code.
- That the CKA block gcn.convs.3 → trunk.0 → trunk.1 → trunk.2 → trunk.3 (0.81–0.95) means the trunk does little feature building. The guide says to look for such blocks but gives no threshold below 0.98, and the sweep contradicts a "redundant" reading.
- That gcn.convs.3 could be narrowed to ~64 without loss because its joint rank-56 truncation is within 1% and rank-44 within 2.3%. Truncating a weight's rank is not the same as training a narrower layer; the guide explicitly says the metrics are descriptive and do not predict a different model.
- That the convs.0 "undertrained" alpha labels can be ignored because of the k=1 / 15-eigenvalue caveat.
- That region_head's rank-3 loss being 1.9% *below* base is noise or mild overfitting rather than a real defect; I have no noise estimate.
- That side_head needing 2 of 3 directions is a harmless property of a 3-way classifier (an overall logit shift does not change the loss), which is my own training knowledge, not something the tool states.
- That the carry doubling 15 → 30 → 60 → 120 is an over-estimate from summing lin_l and lin_r, which both read the same tensor; the guide's "Limits" paragraph says summing over-estimates when producers carry the same information, but does not say whether SAGE's self and neighbour-mean paths count as "the same information".
- That the 23% duplicate units in convs.0.lin_l are a consequence of 15 inputs spread over 128 outputs rather than a training problem.
- Any statement about head conflict; the guide says the tool cannot see it and I have no gradient-cosine numbers.
